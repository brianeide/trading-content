# Experienced derivatives trader/quant interview question

## Question

https://x.com/bennpeifert/status/1884371519968858348 | https://twitter-thread.com/t/1884371519968858348

Experienced derivatives trader/quant interview question: construct a portfolio of VIX options that (approximately) replicates SPX March-June forward starting variance

Extra credit: construct a portfolio of SPX options that does the same

Second extra credit: construct a portfolio of spot-starting variance swaps that does the same

Linking the correct answer here and then will try to explain a bit. This is different from the replication of var(SPX) by trading options on SPX -- because VIX options are already options related to the volatility of SPX

https://x.com/Kevouuz/status/1884708204501668259

Intuitively, the idea behind Carr-Madan is that a nonlinear payoff can be approximated by trading a strip of options such that at each point we get the right slope of the payoff curve with respect to the underlying.

The weights on the VIX options in the replication turn out to be a linear function of the strike, as per the math above. If you want, you can think intuitively of that as because VIX is already volatility, the derivative of x^2 is 2x.

You can make a little spreadsheet and prove this to yourself with a strip of VIX options, show that you get the same payoff as a forward variance swap as a function of volatility.

We're using VIX options here to replicate March-June forward variance. Each VIX option represents a one-month slice of volatility - EG the March VIX future is March-April forward starting volatility (it captures volatility between March expiration and 30 days after).

So we need a strip of March, April and May options to replicate the S&P variance between March and June.

Then in practice you have to take into account the spacing of the VIX options, for certain strike ranges you have a strike every 1 point, for others every 5 points, you need 5 times as many of the latter, etc.

One fun thing about this was a few people were trying to get the various LLM's to answer this question. They all said confident sounding things with formulas and explanations, and were pretty much all wrong, some didn't get the maturities right, none got the strike weighting.

This is exactly what you should expect from LLMs because this question isn't that difficult if you know the math but it's not something you really see written about in public literature. So LLM's are just interpolating from what they read about varswap replication

And saying okay the weights are inverse strike squared, etc. Obviously they can't "reason" and figure out the math, that's not what LLMs do, they are just big nonlinear regressions on words, like parrots regurgitating what they hear.
