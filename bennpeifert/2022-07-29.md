# Musings on risk-taking roles in high finance

https://x.com/bennpeifert/status/1553084045831069697 | https://twitter-thread.com/t/1553084045831069697

Nietszche tells us "the secret for harvesting from existence the greatest fruitfulness and the greatest enjoyment is—to live dangerously! Build your cities on the slopes of Vesuvius! Send your ships into uncharted seas!"

Sounds fabulous. But making or losing fortunes daily also runs many people ragged from emotional strain and self doubt. Here are a couple thoughts on how to embrace it.

Learn to dissociate decisions and outcomes. Most of us do not run dealer desks and our edges are probabilistically small but compound over time if they are real. Short term outcomes are nearly random. Do not beat yourself up over randomness.

Maintain statistically appropriate feedback from outcomes back to process and decisions. Randomness dominates the short term, but over enough time, outcomes of similar decisions become analyzable. Make sure you have a proper framework for performance attribution.

A rigorous investment process built on long-suffered experience reduces chaos dramatically. That does not mean blindly following some model; rather, it means developing a well defined wheelhouse and framework for quickly identifying what is important and how to react.

We were all impatient when we were young. Do not rush into a role where you have to sling capital around without mentorship, even if it is the glittering object. Working on a great team for longer than your primal instinct wants to tolerate is a good idea.

Overcommunicate with your bosses and investors. if they understand how you are thinking about decisions and what your process is, and learn to trust you, they will give you far more rope. if you ride good times with arrogance, in bad times you will die by it.

Actively seek out both mentors and peers in similar roles to share notes, experiences and frustrations with. Isolation allows doubt and stress to fester.

Make a conscious effort to spend enough time and rigor thinking about sizing and portfolio construction. Often people focus too much on trade ideas and not enough on how to put them together in a smart way.

Also, many senior risk takers do not conform at all to the stereotype of the loud, confident execution trader, and are much more analytical and research driven. IME the former is much more visible as the archetype young PMs think they are supposed to strive for.

You can build a team around you that complements your strengths and weaknesses and lets you focus on where you create value.
